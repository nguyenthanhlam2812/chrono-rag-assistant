Retrieval-Augmented Generation for Knowledge-Intensive NLP Tasks

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has become a key methodology in modern natural language processing.
In 2020, Lewis et al. proposed RAG to combine parametric memory (pre-trained language models) and non-parametric memory (dense vector search). This approach significantly mitigated hallucinations in knowledge-intensive tasks.
By 2021, various researchers adapted RAG for question answering and open-domain dialogues.
In 2022, open-source frameworks like LangChain and LlamaIndex were released, making RAG implementation accessible to the wider developer community.
During 2023, Vector Databases like Pinecone, Milvus, and Qdrant saw massive adoption due to RAG requirements.
In early 2024, Microsoft introduced GraphRAG, which leverages Knowledge Graphs rather than simple vector similarity for retrieval, proving highly effective for global summarization tasks.
Today, RAG is a standard architecture for enterprise LLM applications.
